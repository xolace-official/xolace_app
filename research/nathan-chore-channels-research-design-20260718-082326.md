# Design: Trusted Contact Notify — reaching the people who care but don't know

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-07-18
Branch: chore/channels-research

Status: DRAFT
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

Xolace's Phase 2 vision is "proactive support — Xolace reaching toward the user... helping
them be noticed by people in their lives when they are going through something." Today the
only reach-out mechanism is Trusted Bridge (shipped): a user-initiated, AI-drafted message
the user actively composes and sends. It requires the user to have the presence of mind, in
the moment, to open the flow and write to someone. The gap: a user going through something
heavy is often precisely the person least able to take that initiative — and the people who
care about them (who may not be on Xolace at all) have no signal that something is
happening. The research question: can Xolace notice on the user's behalf and, with consent,
let a real person in their life know — without requiring that person to install anything?

## Demand Evidence

- Real quotes exist in feedback/session data (per founder, not yet pulled into this doc)
  expressing "I wish someone had known" / "I told the app but not my [person]" sentiment.
- Founder's own usage observation: some users clearly don't have anyone to tell in the first
  place — that's a distinct, adjacent problem (see Track B below), not evidence against this
  feature, but a reason not to over-scope it as the only proactive-support lever.
- External validation: OpenAI shipped a near-identical "Trusted Contact" feature in ChatGPT
  in May 2026 — opt-in, single designated adult contact, contact must explicitly accept
  before activation, no chat transcript shared, SMS/email alert on detected crisis pattern.
  This is not weak-signal demand; a company with the largest consumer AI surface area judged
  this worth shipping into production. See Cross-Model / Landscape section below.

## Status Quo

Mixed, per founder: sometimes nothing (stays invisible), sometimes the user tells someone
manually after the fact (once the moment that mattered has passed), and separately, some
users don't have anyone in their life to tell at all — that gap is out of scope here and
belongs to a separate "listeners" / peer-counselor track already noted in
`docs/feat-analysis.md`.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Target: a Xolace user who already has at least one person in their life who cares about
them — a parent, partner, friend, sibling — but that person has no way of knowing when the
user is going through something heavy, because the user isn't (or can't be) the one to tell
them in the moment.

Narrowest wedge: reuse the already-shipped Trusted Bridge relationship-capture pattern and
the already-existing `escalation_events` table (which already scores `triggerConfidence`
0.0-1.0 per event) as the trigger signal. Add a new `trustedContacts` table (phone number,
relationship, consent state, `contactAcceptedAt`, `contactOptedOutAt`,
`lastNotifiedAt` for cooldown enforcement). On a high-confidence escalation event, surface
an in-session confirm moment — "Let [name] know you could use them nearby?" — and only send
if the user taps yes, subject to a per-contact cooldown (one notify per contact per N hours,
threshold TBD) so a burst of escalation events can't repeat-fire the same contact. The
contact must also separately accept an invitation SMS via inbound-SMS keyword reply (e.g.
"YES") before they can ever be notified (double opt-in) — this requires an inbound Twilio
webhook, not outbound-only SMS. The same inbound channel handles contact-side opt-out
("STOP"), which sets `contactOptedOutAt` and suppresses all further notifies to that number.
Delivery via Twilio SMS (from `docs/feat-analysis.md`'s Twilio component) — no WhatsApp, no
auto-fire mode, in the first build.

## Constraints

- Xolace's non-negotiable AI-mirror-is-not-a-therapist constraint doesn't directly restrict
  this feature (no diagnosis language involved), but the same restraint applies to the SMS
  copy sent to the contact: no clinical framing, no content disclosure, no "in crisis"
  language — a short, warm, actionable nudge only, matching the vision doc's non-clinical
  posture.
- Must not silently expand `escalation_events`' existing safety-review purpose (human review
  queue) — the notify feature is additive, reads `triggerConfidence`, does not change
  existing escalation handling.
- `trustedContacts` is new PII (a phone number belonging to someone who has never used
  Xolace) — must be handled under the same privacy-first posture as the rest of the schema
  (state accessible for deletion, not swept into anonymization pipelines meant for user
  content only).

## Cross-Model Perspective

Second opinion (Codex) was offered and declined for this session — skipped, proceed on the
landscape search + founder judgment alone.

**Landscape search finding (Phase 2.75):** OpenAI's ChatGPT "Trusted Contact" (launched May
2026) is the closest existing implementation: opt-in, limited to one adult contact per
account, contact must explicitly accept the invitation before the feature activates,
automated detection with human review escalation for serious risk, no chat transcript
shared — just a short check-in message to someone who already knows the user. This directly
validates the double-opt-in guardrail chosen in Approach A below, and reframes the strategic
question from "should this exist" (yes, validated) to "what can Xolace do that ChatGPT's
version structurally can't" (multi-session pattern data, an existing consented-relationship
UI via Trusted Bridge, a non-clinical brand voice already tuned for this exact register).

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Minimal viable — SMS-only, confirm-mode default (RECOMMENDED, chosen)
Effort: M (inbound SMS webhook for accept/opt-out — keyword parsing, signature
verification, phone-number normalization/dedup — pushes this past pure S; see Dependencies). New `trustedContacts` table (name, phone, relationship, `contactAcceptedAt`,
`contactOptedOutAt`, `lastNotifiedAt`). Setup flow: add contact → Twilio sends them a
YES/accept SMS (inbound webhook required to receive the reply); "STOP" on the same channel
opts them out. On `escalation_events` insert with `triggerConfidence` above a threshold
(e.g. 0.75+), subject to a per-contact cooldown, show in-session UI: "Let [name] know you
could use them nearby?" — user taps yes, then Twilio sends the check-in SMS.
No WhatsApp, no auto-fire mode in v1.
Pros: smallest surface area, user always in the loop, contact consent is airtight, matches
the validated ChatGPT precedent almost exactly.
Cons: doesn't help the user who's too overwhelmed to tap yes — the exact case where a safety
net matters most; does not yet ship the auto-mode the founder described wanting eventually.

### Approach B: Full scope — SMS + WhatsApp, both auto and confirm modes
Effort: L. Everything in A, plus a settings-level `notifyMode: "auto" | "confirm"` toggle
(the founder's requested "let the user decide" path), plus WhatsApp Cloud API as an
alternate per-contact channel (4x SMS open rates per `docs/feat-analysis.md`).
Pros: ships the full feature end-to-end in one pass; auto-mode covers the overwhelmed-user
case A misses.
Cons: two messaging integrations plus a mode toggle plus an accept-flow is real surface area
for a feature with zero production usage data — building auto-mode (the least reversible
part: an autonomous system messaging a real third party with no per-event human check)
before confirm-mode has validated the classifier's real-world false-positive rate is
sequencing risk backwards.

### Approach C: Narrower than both — ping yourself first (canary)
Effort: XS. Before involving any real trusted contact, let the user add their own second
phone number (or an explicitly-briefed test contact) and fire the exact same SMS pipeline on
`escalation_events` `triggerConfidence` ≥ threshold, confirm-mode only.
Pros: zero risk of a bad message reaching someone unprepared for it; cheapest way to tune the
confidence threshold against real data.
Cons: doesn't test the actual demand question (will users add a REAL contact, will that
contact show up) — it's a pipeline test, not a product test, delaying the thing that
actually needs to be learned.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A.** Smallest surface area that ships the real product decision (does a real,
double-consented human get reached, and does that change anything) while keeping the
riskiest, least-reversible piece (autonomous messaging with no per-event check) out of v1.
Confirm-mode data (tap-yes rate, contact-accept rate, false-positive reports from users who
say "that wasn't right") is exactly what's needed before Approach B's auto-mode becomes
responsible to build.

## Open Questions

- Exact `triggerConfidence` threshold for surfacing the in-session prompt — needs tuning
  against real escalation data before launch; starting guess 0.75, not locked.
- Copy for both the contact-invitation SMS and the check-in SMS — must avoid clinical
  framing per Xolace's non-negotiable voice constraint; not drafted in this session.
- Where the "add a trusted contact" setup flow lives (Settings vs. a moment inside
  Trusted Bridge) — not decided in this session, deferred to /plan-eng-review.
- Whether `trustedContacts` should cap at one contact (matches ChatGPT's model, simplest) or
  allow more than one — not decided.
- Cooldown window for `lastNotifiedAt` (how many hours between repeat notifies to the same
  contact) — not decided; needs to balance safety-net responsiveness against contact fatigue.
- Exact cooldown/rate-limit tuning values — not decided (default policy below).

**Default revoke policy (until /plan-eng-review revises it):** removing a `trustedContacts`
row from the user side immediately hard-deletes it — any in-flight (sent, unanswered)
invitation becomes inert (the accept webhook finds no matching row and no-ops), and no
further notify can target that number. This is the simplest correct behavior and matches
how the rest of the schema treats user-initiated deletion; it is a default, not a final
design.

## Success Criteria

- Contact accept rate (of invited contacts) as a first-order health signal — low acceptance
  suggests the invitation framing or trust story isn't landing.
- Confirm-tap rate on the in-session prompt (of times it's shown) — measures whether the
  moment and copy feel right to the user in the moment they're offered it.
- Zero unprompted user reports of "that message confused/alarmed someone" — the qualitative
  bar Approach A's guardrails are built to protect.

## Dependencies

- Twilio SMS component (`@convex-dev/twilio`) not yet installed — scoped in
  `docs/feat-analysis.md` but not built.
- **Inbound Twilio SMS webhook** (not just outbound) — required for the contact-accept
  ("YES") and contact opt-out ("STOP") flows: keyword parsing, webhook signature
  verification, and phone-number normalization/dedup are all real sub-tasks, not itemized
  here. Treat effort as M, not S/M, when this reaches /plan-eng-review.
- **A2P 10DLC carrier registration** — US carriers require registered sender identity for
  automated two-way SMS at any real volume; this is a Twilio account-level prerequisite with
  its own lead time (days, not hours) and is not itemized as engineering effort but must be
  started early since it can block launch independent of code readiness.
- Reuses `escalation_events` table and `triggerConfidence` field (already in
  `convex/schema.ts`) — no schema change needed there.
- New `trustedContacts` table — schema addition required.
