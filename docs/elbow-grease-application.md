## Company

### What is the problem you're solving?

There's a massive gap between "everything is fine" (social media performance) and "I need therapy" (clinical intervention). Most people live in that gap — feeling something heavy, anxious, numb, or confused, but not sure what it is or whether it's "bad enough" to seek help. Nothing exists to catch someone at that earliest moment and help them name what's actually going on.

### How did you learn about the problem?

Through a cousin who carried years of struggle silently — she couldn't name what was wrong, and didn't think anyone else would understand either. That silence isn't rare where we're from. In Ghana, naming what you're struggling with isn't just hard, it's discouraged: you're taught to carry it, pray on it, or perform "I'm fine."

### Who has this problem, and how do they deal with it today?

People who don't yet know what they're feeling, or don't feel it's "serious enough" for therapy. Today they deal with it by not dealing with it — performing on social media, or waiting until it compounds into something bigger. The existing options don't meet them here: wellness apps (Calm, Headspace) and therapy platforms (BetterHelp) wait until someone already knows something's wrong. AI companions (Replika, Wysa) catch people earlier, but turn that moment into a parasocial relationship instead of a bridge back to real support.

### What have you built so far?

Live on iOS and Android. An AI-native flow that takes someone from "I don't know what I'm feeling" to a precise reflection of their emotional state in under a minute, then offers a path forward — a guided exercise, connection with others feeling similar, or simply being heard. Built on Expo/React Native, Convex, Claude (Sonnet/Haiku), with voice via ElevenLabs.

### How do you know people need this?

143 users have completed 492 sessions with zero paid acquisition — pure organic pull. In a culture that actively discourages naming what you're struggling with, people are opening the app and writing through it anyway. That's the strongest signal we have: the need isn't hypothetical, people are choosing this against cultural resistance, not because of clever growth tactics.

### Traction, LOIs, user convos, waitlists, revenue

143 users, 492 completed sessions, no paid acquisition, no revenue yet. No LOIs or waitlists at this stage — traction so far is entirely organic usage.

### How will you make money?

Freemium subscription — Xolace+, $9.99/month or $44.99/year. Mental health app spending is already a $6B+ market growing fast across 500M+ English-speaking smartphone users; modest penetration puts consumer subscriptions alone at $100M+ ARR.

### How will you find more customers?

Three channels: partnering with NGOs and organizations already fighting for mental health awareness in our markets, influencer partnerships, and paid acquisition on Google/Apple search ads once we've validated retention economics.

### What is the biggest mistake that you've made so far?

Honestly, nothing crystallized enough yet to point to as *the* mistake — there have been small missteps but nothing we've root-caused as a defining error. We'll come back to this if something surfaces before the deadline.

### Tell us something about your company that's not going well.

Retention. We're early in a market where mental health isn't openly discussed, so a meaningful share of users try Xolace once and don't return — not because the product fails them, but because returning means re-engaging with something their environment has taught them to avoid. We don't fully know yet how much of that is product (onboarding, habit formation) versus market education, and we're actively instrumenting to find out.

### Have you received any funding?

No.

### How will the next LLM model release affect your business?

We're built AI-native from the ground up — every model call lives behind a single cognition layer, not scattered across features. As models get better, our reflection quality, latency, and cost all improve directly without re-architecting. We benefit from the frontier moving forward; we don't have to chase it.

### How do you use AI in your workflows today? Please share specifics.

We use Claude Code and Windsurf to prototype new features fast — going from idea to a testable build in the app is a matter of hours, not sprints. On the product side, session data (emotion tags, mirror confirmation rates, clarification turns, path choices) feeds back into our own AI pipeline, which helps us surface which parts of the flow are working and where users are dropping off, so product decisions are informed by structured signal rather than guesswork.

### Who are your competitors or alternatives, and why will you win?

AI companions (Replika, Wysa), passive wellness apps (Calm, Headspace), and therapy platforms (BetterHelp). Wellness and therapy wait until someone already knows something's wrong — too late to catch what's compounding. AI companions catch the moment early but use it to become the relationship itself instead of a bridge to real support. We believe the earliest feeling is the actual leverage point, but only if it leads back to human connection instead of replacing it — that's the wedge they don't have.

### What's something you believe about your market or approach that a smart, informed person would disagree with? Why do you believe it anyway?

We deliberately refuse to let the AI form a relationship with the user — no persona, no back-and-forth companionship, no memory that makes it feel like a friend. Most people building in this space treat engagement and attachment to the AI as the goal, because it drives retention. We think that's the wrong optimization: an AI can never actually feel what a human feels, and building emotional dependency on something that can't reciprocate is a dead end, or worse, a replacement for real connection. We'd rather use AI's capability to make the human layer work — mirroring, clarifying, then pointing back to peers or real support — even though that's a harder retention story to tell than a sticky companion.
