# Library: trusted source roster and reuse terms

Research for [#384](https://github.com/xolace-official/xolace_app/issues/384) (map [#383](https://github.com/xolace-official/xolace_app/issues/383)).
Checked 2026-09-23 against each organisation's own terms/copyright page. Not legal advice. Re-check before launch, because these pages change.

**Constraints from the map rulings:** always credit the source. Some entries are adapted, some verbatim. English and region-neutral at launch.

**The fact that drives most rows:** Xolace is a commercial app (it has paid subscriptions via RevenueCat). Almost every charity licence is either "personal use only" or "non-commercial". So even a free Library tab inside a paid app is commercial use. Only public-domain and OGL content is safe without asking.

### African-market sources

- **Mentally Aware Nigeria Initiative (MANI)**, [mentallyaware.org](https://mentallyaware.org/). This is the largest youth mental-health network in West Africa (about 1,500 volunteers, 18 state chapters, per [findahelpline](https://findahelpline.com/organizations/mentally-aware-nigeria-initiative-mani) and [MHIN](https://www.mhinnovation.net/organisations/mentally-aware-nigeria-initiative-mani)). It covers students and young people, anxiety, relationships, school and work. The site blocks automated fetch and I found no published reuse licence, so all rights are reserved by default. **This is a partnership target, not a content source.** It is valuable for local voice, Nigerian life-slice stories and local signposting.
- **Ghana Mental Health Authority**, [mhaghana.com](https://mha-ghana.com/). This is a government regulator created under the Mental Health Act 2012 (Act 846). It is useful for **Ghana signposting only**; it has no consumer self-help library worth seeding from.
- **WHO / WHO AFRO** (row 17) is the realistic region-neutral content base for African users, subject to the NC permission.
- No strong African source with an open content licence exists. The African slice has to come through partnership (MANI, plus possibly Ghanaian university counselling units) or through Xolace-written pieces that cite public-domain or OGL facts.

## Recommendation

**Safe now: verbatim or adapted, no permission needed**
1. **NIMH**: public domain, and our best clinical explainer base. Cite NIMH. Don't use their images.
2. **SAMHSA**: public domain. Check each item for third-party material. Cite SAMHSA.
3. **NHS (OGL v3.0)**: the best practical self-help base. **Default to adapt-only**, which means generalising the UK signposting and using the "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0" line. The adapted version must not be branded "NHS", so a "Source: NHS" chip is **not allowed on adapted entries**. Verbatim is only realistic through the Syndicated Content API because of the 7-day refresh rule.

**Conditionally safe: needs a light-touch permission email first**
4. **Anna Freud**: free use with credit plus an on-screen link, but it bars selling derivatives. Verbatim in a free, unpaywalled Library is defensible; get written confirmation before placing it behind the paywall or adapting it.
5. **NAMI**: gives reprint permission routinely (credit, not for resale, notify editor@nami.org). Send the request before use.
6. **Mind**: quotes of 200 words or fewer with "© Mind" are OK today. For anything longer, use their paid permissions or e-licence.

**Permission or partnership first: do not use until written consent**
Mental Health Foundation, YoungMinds, Student Minds, Samaritans, Rethink, HelpGuide, Jed, MHA (beyond credited excerpts), Beyond Blue, ReachOut, CCI, WHO (NC in a paid app), and MANI.
Priority outreach, by fit to our life-slices and story needs:
- **Mind**: depth plus lived-experience stories; its e-licence is an existing product.
- **Student Minds**: the student slice.
- **YoungMinds**: young-people stories.
- **MANI**: Nigerian voice and signposting.
- **WHO**: region-neutral illustrated self-help.

**Ways to still use a restricted source now:** link out ("Read more at Mind"). Linking is allowed everywhere except Samaritans, which wants links notified. Or write an original Xolace piece that cites the source as reading. Facts aren't copyrightable; wording is.

## Implications for the Library data model (for #392)

- Each entry needs `sourceOrg`, `sourceUrl`, `licence` (e.g. `public-domain` | `OGL-3.0` | `permission` | `quote-200w`), `mode` (`verbatim` | `adapted` | `original-citing`), `attributionText` (the exact required string), and `retrievedAt`.
- Verbatim NHS entries need a refresh job, or they should be adapted instead.
- The attribution string has to be rendered exactly as each licence requires, which differs per source. A generic "Source: X" chip is not enough.
- Crisis and helpline signposting should come from Xolace's own region-aware config, not from the copied text. That is what makes the UK and US content generalisable.
