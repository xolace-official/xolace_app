Xolace · Engineering note

How the fire finds
who feels like you

The peer-matching feature for Xolace+ doesn't just match tags. It matches meaning — pairing your reflection with strangers who felt the same thing, even when they used completely different words.

The idea in one line

Old way matched labels. The new way matches feelings.

Every reflection carries tags like primaryEmotion or granularLabel. The free matcher walks those tags. That's fast, but two people can feel the exact same thing and never share a single tag — so they never meet. Semantic matching turns the text itself into a point in "meaning-space" and finds its nearest neighbours.

Tag cascade — free

"match by granularLabel → then broad emotion → then intensity" Reliable, but blind to anything the tags didn't capture.

Semantic search — Xolace+

"match by what the words actually mean" Finds echoes that share no tags at all — only the underlying feeling.

How the data actually moves

Two quiet machines, running at different times

Nothing happens live while you wait. One machine slowly fills a pool of anonymous feelings. A second machine searches that pool for you the moment you ask to see peers.

Write path

Filling the pool

Runs whenever someone chooses to share their reflection. It never blocks them.

  1. 1

    A session ends, and the person opts to contribute

    Their choice schedules an anonymizing job in the background. They've already moved on.

  2. 2

    Anonymize — strip identity, keep the feeling

    We use the first-person distilled text, drop every link back to the person, and blur the timestamp to the nearest day so it can't be traced.

    ✦ Crisis gate — crisis sessions never enter the pool, full stop
  3. 3

    Save the anonymous reflection

    contribute() writes one clean row: the words plus its emotional fingerprint. Then it schedules the embedding.

    anonymized text + emotion tags
  4. ✦

    The fire reads it — text becomes a vector

    The RAG engine sends the text to an embedding model. It comes back as a list of 1,536 numbers — coordinates that place this feeling in meaning-space.

    text → [0.02, −0.91, … ] → vector pool
Read path

Finding your echoes

Runs the instant a Xolace+ member chooses the "peers" path.

  1. 1

    You confirm your mirror and tap "see peers"

    Because you're on Xolace+, that tap quietly schedules a search — while you're still walking to the peers screen.

  2. ✦

    Your mirror becomes a vector too

    The same fire turns your reflection into coordinates, then looks for the closest points already floating in the pool.

    your mirror → search(pool, limit 8)
  3. 3

    Keep only the ones that truly fit

    From the 8 nearest, we drop anything no longer active and anything whose intensity is more than 3 apart from yours — then keep the top 4.

    8 nearest → intensity ±3 → top 4
  4. 4

    The screen fills itself in

    The match query is reactive: it shows tag-based results first, then swaps in the semantic matches the moment they're ready — no reload, no spinner.

    tag results → semantic matches (live)
Why "meaning, not tags" matters
"I feel like I'm slowly disappearing and no one would notice."
MATCHES≈
"Lately I feel invisible, like I could vanish and life goes on."

These two share no tags — different words, different labels. Tag matching would keep them apart. Semantic matching sees they're the same ache and puts them beside each other.

The same thing, explained to a 10-year-old

The jar of feeling-notes by the campfire

🔥

Imagine a warm campfire. People sit around it and, when they're ready, whisper how they feel onto a little note — then drop it into a big glass jar. They take their name off first, so it's just the feeling.

✨

A magic firefly reads each note and gives it a secret color-code — not the words, but the feeling behind them. Two notes that feel the same get almost the same color, even if the words are totally different.

🫙

All those color-coded notes float together in the jar. That's the pool. It just quietly grows as more people share.

🤝

Now you whisper how you feel. The firefly gives your note its color, then reaches into the jar and pulls out the few notes whose colors are closest to yours.

🎚️

It skips the ones that feel way stronger or way calmer than you right now, and hands you the four that feel most like your feeling.

🪞

You read them and think, "someone else felt exactly this." You're alone by the fire — but not the only one who's ever felt this way.

Privacy by construction: pool reflections carry no link to a person, timestamps are blurred to a day, and crisis sessions are excluded before they ever reach the embedding step.
Graceful by default: if the search isn't ready or finds nothing, the screen simply keeps the tag-based matches. The free experience is unchanged, byte for byte.
